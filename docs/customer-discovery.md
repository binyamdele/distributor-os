# Customer Discovery — Medium-Sized Construction-Material Distributors, Addis Ababa

The purpose of these interviews is to find out whether the manual work between a customer
inquiry and a fulfilled order is painful enough that a distributor would pay to remove it.

The purpose is **not** to describe the product. Describing the product early produces polite
agreement, which is worthless. Ask about what happened last week, not about what they would
like in theory.

---

## How to run the interview

- Target: the owner, the sales manager, and at least one salesperson — separately where
  possible. They will not describe the same process.
- Length: 30–40 minutes.
- Ask for specifics and numbers. "How many quotations did you send yesterday?" beats "do you
  send many quotations?"
- Ask to see the actual artefacts: the price list, the quotation template, the WhatsApp thread,
  the notebook. What people show is more accurate than what they say.
- Do not pitch until the last five minutes. Then pitch a narrow claim and watch the reaction to
  price, not to the idea.

---

## Core questions

1. Where do customer inquiries arrive?
2. How many inquiries do you receive daily?
3. Who prepares quotations?
4. How long does one quotation take?
5. Where are current prices stored?
6. How frequently do prices change?
7. How do salespeople check inventory?
8. How do you track quotations that do not convert?
9. How are discounts approved?
10. How does finance know which customer paid?
11. Do customers buy on credit?
12. How do you track overdue payments?
13. How does the warehouse receive orders?
14. How are deliveries coordinated?
15. What does management struggle to see every day?
16. If we could remove one repetitive task tomorrow, what would it be?

---

## Follow-up probes that tend to expose the real cost

These matter because they convert "it is a bit annoying" into a number.

- **On quotation effort:** walk me through the last quotation you sent. What did you have to
  look up, and where? How many times did you switch between things?
- **On price freshness:** has a quotation ever gone out with an out-of-date price? What
  happened? Who absorbed the difference?
- **On stock:** has a customer ever accepted a quotation for something you did not actually
  have? How often?
- **On lost quotes:** of the quotations you sent last month, how many did you follow up on? How
  do you know which ones you missed?
- **On approvals:** what is the largest discount a salesperson can give without asking? What
  happens when someone exceeds it — before or after the customer is told?
- **On payment:** when a customer sends a bank screenshot, who checks it and how long does the
  order wait? Has an order ever been released before payment was actually confirmed?
- **On credit:** who decides whether a customer can buy on credit? Where is that recorded? Has
  a suspended customer ever been sold to anyway?
- **On the handoff:** how does the warehouse learn that an order is ready to prepare? What
  happens when that message is missed?
- **On language:** do customers write in Amharic, English, or both? Do product names come in
  consistently?

---

## What to observe rather than ask

- Whether the price list is a spreadsheet, a printout, or someone's memory.
- Whether quotations are numbered, and whether the numbering is reliable.
- Whether the same customer name is spelled three different ways.
- How many WhatsApp groups the sales team is in.
- Whether the owner is personally in the approval loop for routine discounts.

## Signals worth recording after each interview

For every interview, record:

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| Company, size, number of salespeople | |
| Inquiries per day (their number, not an estimate) | |
| Minutes per quotation (their number) | |
| Where the pain was described **unprompted** | The strongest signal in the whole interview |
| Which existing tool, if any, already solves it | Excel counts as a tool that works |
| Willingness to pilot | Yes / no / conditional on what |
| Willingness to pay | The amount they said, and who said it |
| Who would have to approve a purchase | |

An interview where the interviewee describes the pain before you mention it is worth more than
five where they agree with your description of it. Record which happened.

## What would disconfirm the hypothesis

Write these down honestly after each interview:

- The quotation process is already fast because prices rarely change.
- Quotations are low-volume, and the bottleneck is elsewhere (supply, cash, transport).
- Salespeople like the current process because informality gives them latitude.
- The owner considers the manual work a control mechanism rather than a cost.
- An existing system — even a spreadsheet — is good enough that nobody would switch.

If several interviews produce these, that is a finding, not a failure. Record it in
`validation-scorecard.md`.
